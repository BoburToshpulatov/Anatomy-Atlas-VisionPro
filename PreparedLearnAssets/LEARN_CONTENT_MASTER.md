## Heart

### Organ: Heart
- Chapter number: 1
- Chapter title: Meet the Heart
- Asset name: `heart_overview.png`
- Recommended layout direction: `image-left`
- Learning goal: Understand what the heart is and why it is central to survival.
- Main explanation: The heart is a muscular pump that keeps blood moving through the lungs and the rest of the body. Every beat delivers oxygen, nutrients, and chemical signals to tissues while carrying waste away. In Learn Mode, the heart is introduced first as a complete organ before its internal parts are broken down.
- 3 key facts:
  - The heart sits slightly left of center in the chest.
  - It works continuously, beating thousands of times each day.
  - Its main job is to keep oxygen-poor and oxygen-rich blood moving in the correct direction.
- Short caption for the image: A full view of the heart as the body’s central pump.
- Quiz idea connected to this chapter: Ask which organ acts as the main muscular pump of the circulatory system.
- Labels used in illustration:
  - Heart

### Organ: Heart
- Chapter number: 2
- Chapter title: The Four Chambers
- Asset name: `heart_chambers.png`
- Recommended layout direction: `image-right`
- Learning goal: Learn the names and roles of the four chambers.
- Main explanation: The heart has four chambers that separate incoming and outgoing blood. The right side receives oxygen-poor blood and sends it to the lungs, while the left side receives oxygen-rich blood and pushes it to the body. Understanding the chambers helps learners follow blood flow more easily in later chapters.
- 3 key facts:
  - The atria are the upper receiving chambers.
  - The ventricles are the lower pumping chambers.
  - The left ventricle has the thickest muscular wall because it pumps blood to the whole body.
- Short caption for the image: The heart’s four chambers organize how blood enters and leaves the organ.
- Quiz idea connected to this chapter: Ask which chamber pumps oxygen-rich blood into the systemic circulation.
- Labels used in illustration:
  - Right Atrium
  - Right Ventricle
  - Left Atrium
  - Left Ventricle

### Organ: Heart
- Chapter number: 3
- Chapter title: The One-Way Valves
- Asset name: `heart_valves.png`
- Recommended layout direction: `image-left`
- Learning goal: Understand how valves keep blood moving in only one direction.
- Main explanation: Valves open when pressure pushes blood forward and close to prevent backward flow. This one-way system helps each chamber work efficiently and keeps circulation coordinated. When valves fail to close properly, the heart has to work harder.
- 3 key facts:
  - Valves act like doors that open and shut with pressure changes.
  - The atrioventricular valves sit between atria and ventricles.
  - The semilunar valves sit at the exits to the major arteries.
- Short caption for the image: Heart valves direct blood forward and stop backflow.
- Quiz idea connected to this chapter: Ask which valve separates the left atrium from the left ventricle.
- Labels used in illustration:
  - Mitral Valve
  - Tricuspid Valve
  - Aortic Valve
  - Pulmonary Valve

- Asset name: `heart_coronary_vessels.png`

- Learning goal: Understand that the heart muscle needs its own blood vessels.

- 3 key facts:

  - Blocked coronary arteries can damage heart tissue quickly.
- Short caption for the image: Coronary vessels feed the heart muscle that powers every heartbeat.

### Organ: Heart
- Chapter number: 6
- Chapter title: The Electrical System
- Asset name: `heart_conduction_system.png`
- Recommended layout direction: `image-right`
- Learning goal: Learn how electrical signals coordinate the heartbeat.
- Main explanation: The heart contracts because specialized cells generate and spread electrical signals. The sinoatrial node starts the rhythm, the atrioventricular node briefly delays it, and the signal then moves through conducting fibers into the ventricles. This timing ensures the chambers squeeze in an efficient sequence.
- 3 key facts:
  - The sinoatrial node acts as the natural pacemaker.
  - A short delay lets the ventricles fill before they contract.
  - Conducting fibers help the ventricles squeeze from the bottom upward.
- Short caption for the image: Electrical signals guide the order and timing of each heartbeat.
- Quiz idea connected to this chapter: Ask which structure normally starts the heartbeat.
- Labels used in illustration:
  - SA Node
  - AV Node
  - Bundle Branches
  - Purkinje Fibers
